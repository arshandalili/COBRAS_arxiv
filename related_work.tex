\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
\noindent\textbf{Theory of activation steering and Schr\"odinger Bridge.}~Several recent works interpret activation interventions through explicit mathematical objectives. Linear Activation Transport~\citep{rodriguez2025controlling} constructs maps between source and target activation distributions, LiSeCo~\citep{cheng2024linearly} formulates control through linear separability and constrained optimization, and ODESteer~\citep{zhaoodesteer} relates steering to density-ratio estimation and continuous-time dynamics. Taimeskhanov et al.~\citep{taimeskhanov2026towards} analyze how additive steering strength affects next-token probabilities, concept expression, and cross-entropy. These perspectives provide useful formal tools for reasoning about activation interventions. However, their guarantees are typically derived for surrogate objectives and under specific geometric constraints. Also, the Schr\"odinger Bridge problem has been widely adopted for generative modeling via forward--backward SDE solvers~\citep{de2021diffusion, chen2021likelihood, shi2023diffusion}, unpaired image translation~\citep{kim2023unpaired}, and single-cell dynamics~\citep{tong2023simulation}, and extended to Riemannian manifolds~\citep{thornton2022riemannian}. Unlike these solvers, which train neural drifts, \Cobras fits the bridge with Sinkhorn on contrastive activations and is the first to apply SB to LLM activation steering.

\noindent\textbf{Activation steering approaches.}~Early and widely used steering approaches~\cite{zou2023representation, li2023inference, rimsky2024steering} identify behavior-relevant directions in activation space and apply additive edits. More recent methods refine this basic recipe by accounting for activation geometry or distributional structure, for example through minimally affine maps~\citep{singh2024representation}, norm-preserving rotations~\citep{pham2024householder}, transport-based maps~\citep{rodriguez2025controlling}, hyperspherical interventions~\citep{you2026spherical}, or continuous dynamical updates~\citep{zhaoodesteer}. Several methods adapt the intervention to the current activation~\citep{pham2024householder, you2026spherical, zhaoodesteer, wang2025truthflow}, but none checks whether the contrastive data support steering a given input. Prior work shows that steering can be unreliable across inputs, prompts, and models, induce off-target side effects, and degrade performance on inputs that need no steering~\citep{tan2024analysing, da2025steering, raedler2025necessity, stickland2024steering}. \Cobras instead derives a query-adaptive steering field and a support gate from the same fitted bridge, improving in-distribution performance while largely preserving OOD performance.

Building on this idea, Inference-Time Intervention (ITI)~\citep{li2023inference} steers selected attention-head activations along truthfulness-related directions, while Contrastive Activation Addition (CAA)~\citep{rimsky2024steering} constructs steering vectors from differences between positive and negative examples and adds them to residual-stream activations during generation. More recent methods move beyond simple additive interventions by considering richer activation geometry and distributional structure: Minimally Modified Counterfactuals (MiMiC)~\citep{singh2024representation} applies minimally invasive affine transformations to match counterfactual activation statistics, Householder Pseudo-Rotation (HPR)~\citep{pham2024householder} uses norm-preserving pseudo-rotations, Linear Activation Transport (Linear-AcT)~\citep{rodriguez2025controlling} formulates steering as a transport map between source and target activation distributions, SphericalSteering~\citep{you2026spherical} performs steering on the hypersphere to preserve activation magnitudes, and ODESteer~\citep{zhaoodesteer} interprets steering as a continuous dynamical process rather than a one-shot edit.
