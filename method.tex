\section{Background}
\label{sec:bg}

\noindent\textbf{Setup and notation.}~We fix a decoder-only transformer of residual width $d$ and a layer index $\ell$, and write $\boldsymbol{h}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$ for the layer-$\ell$ residual-stream activation at the last token position. Two populations of inputs are given, one exhibiting a desired behavior and one an undesired behavior, with activation distributions $\pplus$ and $\pminus$ observed through i.i.d.\ samples $\mathcal{D}^{+}=\{\boldsymbol{h}^{+}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N_{+}}\sim\pplus$ and $\mathcal{D}^{-}=\{\boldsymbol{h}^{-}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N_{-}}\sim\pminus$, whose empirical measures we denote $\pplushat$ and $\pminushat$. At inference, a steering map is applied to the layer-$\ell$ activation of each decoded token, beginning with the last prompt token, and $\hquery$ denotes the activation currently being steered. The map should move $\hquery$ toward regions of high density under $\pplus$ and low density under $\pminus$, and it modifies no other activation. Vectors and vector fields are set in bold.

\noindent\textbf{Spherical geometry of activations.}~Residual-stream norms concentrate tightly across inputs at a fixed layer, both empirically~\citep{pham2024householder, you2026spherical} and as a consequence of the residual architecture~\citep{yang2023spectral, xie2026controlled}. Figure~\ref{fig:norm-concentration} shows this for the four models we study, for which the coefficient of variation of the layer-normalized last-token norm lies between $2.5\%$ and $7.3\%$, and Appendix~\ref{appendix:norm_concentration} reports the same measurement layer by layer and for four further architectures. We therefore model the state space as a sphere $\Sph$ and restore the original norm of $\hquery$ after steering, so that the intervention changes only the direction of the activation.

\noindent\textbf{Operations on $\Sph$.}~For simplicity, we work in unit-sphere coordinates, so $\Sph=\{\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\lVert\boldsymbol{x}\rVert=1\}$. We write $\Pi_{\boldsymbol{x}}=\boldsymbol{I}-\boldsymbol{x}\boldsymbol{x}^{\top}$ for the projector onto the tangent space at $\boldsymbol{x}$, $\nablaS f=\Pi_{\boldsymbol{x}}\nabla f$ for the Riemannian gradient, and $d_{\Sph}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})=\arccos(\boldsymbol{x}^{\top}\boldsymbol{y})$ for the geodesic distance. The exponential map $\exp_{\boldsymbol{x}}(\boldsymbol{v})$ moves from $\boldsymbol{x}$ along the tangent vector $\boldsymbol{v}$ for a geodesic arc of length $\lVert\boldsymbol{v}\rVert$, and its inverse $\log_{\boldsymbol{x}}(\boldsymbol{y})$, defined for $\boldsymbol{y}\neq-\boldsymbol{x}$, is the tangent vector at $\boldsymbol{x}$ that points toward $\boldsymbol{y}$ with norm $d_{\Sph}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})$:
\begin{equation}
\exp_{\boldsymbol{x}}(\boldsymbol{v})
= \cos\!\left(\lVert\boldsymbol{v}\rVert\right)\boldsymbol{x}
+ \sin\!\left(\lVert\boldsymbol{v}\rVert\right)\frac{\boldsymbol{v}}{\lVert\boldsymbol{v}\rVert},
\qquad
\log_{\boldsymbol{x}}(\boldsymbol{y}) = \frac{\theta}{\sin\theta}\,\Pi_{\boldsymbol{x}}\boldsymbol{y},
\quad \theta = d_{\Sph}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}).
\label{eq:expmap}
\end{equation}
When the tangent length or angular separation is zero, we evaluate the $\log$ and $\exp$ maps at their continuous limits. These operations specify how to move activations on $\Sph$. %The next section discusses how to construct a steering field from the desired and undesired activation populations.}

\section{Activation Steering as a Schr\"{o}dinger Bridge}
\label{sec:method}
In this section, we develop \Cobras, starting from a Schr\"{o}dinger Bridge formulation of activation steering. In Section~\ref{subsec:sb}, we formulate transport between the undesired and desired activation populations as a constrained optimization problem and characterize its solution through two Schr\"{o}dinger potentials. The gradient of the log-ratio of the potentials motivates our steering field, while their product determines the bridge's activation density at each transport time, motivating a criterion for assessing support for an intervention. Building on this formulation, Section~\ref{subsec:estimation} constructs a steering field using an endpoint transport plan estimated from contrastive data. Section~\ref{subsec:strength} introduces adaptive strength controls, and Section~\ref{subsec:procedure} combines these components into the inference procedure. Appendix~\ref{apx:ldr-formalize} establishes the kernel log-density-ratio limit of \Cobras and discusses related interpretations of existing steering methods. Figure~\ref{fig:overview} provides the overview of the method.

\subsection{Schr\"{o}dinger Bridge and transport objective}
\label{subsec:sb}
We first study steering at the population level. Recall that we seek a transport from the undesired activation distribution $\pminus$ to the desired distribution $\pplus$ on $\Sph$. This characterization will provide us with a direction of motion that can be evaluated at a given activation during inference. A trajectory describes how one activation moves during steering, with $t\in[0,1]$ indexing steering progress over time. Let $P$ denote a probability distribution over these continuous trajectories, and let $P_t$ denote the resulting activation distribution at time $t$, so that we require $P_0=\pminus$ and $P_1=\pplus$.

The endpoint distributions alone do not specify how activations should move between them. To select a trajectory distribution, we introduce a reference trajectory distribution $Q$ and minimize deviation from it, measured by Kullback--Leibler (KL) divergence. We choose $Q$ to be Brownian motion on $\Sph$, with diffusion scale $\sigma^2$ and a uniform initial distribution, providing a geometric reference with no preferred starting location or direction. The Schr\"{o}dinger Bridge (SB) is the trajectory distribution closest to this reference subject to the prescribed endpoints~\citep{schrodinger1932theorie, de2021diffusion}:
\begin{equation}
P^\star=\operatorname*{arg\,min}\nolimits_{P}\KL(P\,\Vert\,Q)
\qquad\text{s.t.}\qquad P_0=\pminus,\quad P_1=\pplus.
\label{eq:sb}
\end{equation}
We next characterize the solution to this problem and leverage it for steering individual activations. Under mild regularity assumptions stated in Appendix~\ref{apx:static}, the optimal solution to Eq.~\eqref{eq:sb} admits the factorized representation~\citep{leonard2013survey} stated as follows:
\begin{equation}
P^\star(\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}_{[0,1]})
=
\varphi(\boldsymbol{x}_0,0)\,
\psi(\boldsymbol{x}_1,1)\,
Q(\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}_{[0,1]}),
\label{eq:sb-factorization}
\end{equation}
where $\boldsymbol{x}_{[0,1]}=(\boldsymbol{x}_t)_{t\in[0,1]}$ denotes a continuous activation trajectory. The positive functions $\varphi,\psi:\Sph\times[0,1]\to(0,\infty)$ are called \emph{Schr\"{o}dinger potentials}. Their endpoint potentials $\varphi(\boldsymbol{x},0)$ and $\psi(\boldsymbol{x},1)$ are determined jointly to satisfy $P^\star_0=\pminus$ and $P^\star_1=\pplus$. The reference Brownian dynamics propagate the former forward and the latter backward in time, determining $\varphi(\boldsymbol{x},t)$ and $\psi(\boldsymbol{x},t)$ at intermediate time $t$. For $P=P^\star$, we write $P_t(\boldsymbol{x})$ for the density of the marginal $P_t$ with respect to spherical volume. Since the reference has uniform time marginals, this density is proportional to the product of the potentials:
\begin{equation}
P_t(\boldsymbol{x})
\propto \varphi(\boldsymbol{x},t)\psi(\boldsymbol{x},t).
\label{eq:product}
\end{equation}

The bridge $P^\star$ describes a distribution over stochastic activation trajectories. To motivate a deterministic steering field, we consider its probability-flow ODE, whose solutions have distribution $P_t$ at every time $t$ when initialized with $\boldsymbol{x}_0\sim\pminus$~\citep{song2020score, de2022riemannian}. For the Brownian reference $Q$, this ODE takes the form
\begin{equation}
\displaystyle
\frac{\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}_t}{\mathrm{d}t}
=
\frac{\sigma^2}{2}\nablaS
\log\frac{\psi(\boldsymbol{x}_t,t)}
{\varphi(\boldsymbol{x}_t,t)}.
\label{eq:ldr}
\end{equation}
The drift of this ODE motivates constructing a steering field from the gradient of the log-ratio of the potentials. To develop a sample-based construction, we first estimate discrete counterparts of the endpoint potentials $\varphi(\boldsymbol{x},0)$ and $\psi(\boldsymbol{x},1)$. To this end, we express the trajectory optimization in Eq.~\eqref{eq:sb} as a static problem over the joint distribution of starting and ending activations and solve its discrete approximation using the celebrated Sinkhorn algorithm~\citep{cuturi2013sinkhorn}.

Let $P_{01}$ and $Q_{01}$ denote the joint distributions of
starting and ending activations under $P$ and $Q$, respectively.
The optimization in Eq.~\eqref{eq:sb} reduces to the static
Schr\"{o}dinger Bridge problem:
\begin{equation}
\displaystyle
\min_{P}\,
\underset{\text{s.t.}\;P_{01}(\cdot,\Sph)=\pminus,\;P_{01}(\Sph,\cdot)=\pplus}
{\KL(P_{01}\,\Vert\,Q_{01})}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
P^\star_{01}(\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x},\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{y})
=
\varphi(\boldsymbol{x},0)\,
\psi(\boldsymbol{y},1)\,
Q_{01}(\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x},\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{y}).
\label{eq:static-sb}
\end{equation}
Here $\boldsymbol{x}$ and $\boldsymbol{y}$ denote the starting
and ending activations, and the two constraints prescribe
their marginal distributions. Enforcing these constraints
yields coupled equations for the endpoint potentials
$\varphi(\boldsymbol{x},0)$ and $\psi(\boldsymbol{y},1)$.
In practice, however, the endpoint distributions $\pminus$ and
$\pplus$ are observed only through the finite contrastive sample
sets $\mathcal{D}^-$ and $\mathcal{D}^+$. To estimate the endpoint
potentials from these data, we therefore discretize the static
problem on the contrastive samples.

The discrete counterpart of $P_{01}$ is a joint endpoint distribution $\widehat{P}_{01}$, represented by a nonnegative matrix whose entry $\widehat{P}_{01,ji}$ assigns probability mass to the sample pair $(\boldsymbol{h}_j^-,\boldsymbol{h}_i^+)$. Its marginals must match the empirical endpoint distributions, requiring $\sum_i\widehat{P}_{01,ji}=1/N_-$ and $\sum_j\widehat{P}_{01,ji}=1/N_+$. We also replace $Q_{01}$ with a discrete reference
distribution $\widehat{Q}_{01}$ on the same sample pairs.
We construct this reference using a geodesic surrogate
for the Brownian transition density:
\begin{equation}
\displaystyle
\widehat{Q}_{01,ji}
=
\frac{e^{-c_{ji}}}{\sum_{j',i'}e^{-c_{j'i'}}},
\qquad
c_{ji}
=
\frac{d_{\Sph}(\boldsymbol{h}_j^-,\boldsymbol{h}_i^+)^2}
{2\varepsilon},
\label{eq:discrete-reference}
\end{equation}
where $\varepsilon>0$ controls the spread of the reference
weights across sample pairs. The discrete
Schr\"{o}dinger Bridge therefore solves
\begin{equation}
\displaystyle
\widehat{P}_{01}^{\star}
=
\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{\widehat{P}_{01}}
\underset{
\text{s.t.}\;
\sum_i\widehat{P}_{01,ji}=1/N_-,
\;\sum_j\widehat{P}_{01,ji}=1/N_+
}{
\KL(\widehat{P}_{01}\,\Vert\,\widehat{Q}_{01})
}.
\label{eq:discrete-sb}
\end{equation}

As in Eq.~\eqref{eq:static-sb}, the optimal joint endpoint
distribution factorizes into the reference distribution
and two endpoint potentials:
\begin{equation}
\displaystyle
\widehat{P}_{01,ji}^{\star}
=
\widehat{\varphi}_{0,j}\,
\widehat{\psi}_{1,i}\,
\widehat{Q}_{01,ji}.
\label{eq:discrete-factors}
\end{equation}
Here $\widehat{\varphi}_{0,j}$ and $\widehat{\psi}_{1,i}$
are discrete counterparts of
$\varphi(\boldsymbol{x},0)$ and $\psi(\boldsymbol{y},1)$,
associated with undesired sample $j$ and desired sample $i$,
respectively. We compute these potentials using the
Sinkhorn algorithm~\citep{cuturi2013sinkhorn}, which
alternately updates $\widehat{\varphi}_{0,j}$  and $\widehat{\psi}_{1,i}$ to enforce
the undesired  the desired marginals, respectively.

The drift of the probability-flow ODE in Eq.~\eqref{eq:ldr} motivates constructing our steering field from the gradient of a log-ratio of potentials. In Section~\ref{subsec:estimation}, we use the fitted endpoint potentials to construct a steering field that can be evaluated at a given activation during inference.


\subsection{Constructing the steering field}
\label{subsec:estimation}
The drift of the ODE in Eq.~\eqref{eq:ldr} motivates constructing a steering field from the gradient of a log-ratio of potentials. However, the fitted endpoint potentials $\widehat{\varphi}_{0,j}$ and $\widehat{\psi}_{1,i}$ are associated with discrete contrastive samples. To obtain spatial gradients at the current activation $\boldsymbol{x}\in\Sph$, we construct two weighted kernel sums from these potentials and the reference distribution $\widehat{Q}_{01}$. These sums incorporate the samples' proximity to the activation through the kernel
\begin{equation}
\displaystyle
K_b(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{h})
=\exp\!\left[-\frac{d_{\Sph}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{h})^2}{2b}\right],
\qquad b>0,
\label{eq:kernel}
\end{equation}
where $\boldsymbol{h}\in\mathcal{D}^-\cup\mathcal{D}^+$ and $b$ is the bandwidth. Combining these proximity weights with the fitted endpoint potentials and reference distribution, we define
\begin{align}
\widetilde{\varphi}_b(\boldsymbol{x})
&=\sum_j
\left[\sum_i \widehat{\psi}_{1,i}\,\widehat{Q}_{01,ji}\,
K_b(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{h}_i^+)\right]
K_b(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{h}_j^-),
\label{eq:local-phi}\\
\widetilde{\psi}_b(\boldsymbol{x})
&=\sum_i
\left[\sum_j \widehat{\varphi}_{0,j}\,\widehat{Q}_{01,ji}\,
K_b(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{h}_j^-)\right]
K_b(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{h}_i^+).
\label{eq:local-psi}
\end{align}
In $\widetilde{\psi}_b$, the bracketed coefficient for each desired sample aggregates contributions from undesired samples, weighted by their fitted endpoint potentials, their proximity to $\boldsymbol{x}$, and their reference weights connecting them to that desired sample. The outer kernel additionally accounts for the desired sample's proximity to $\boldsymbol{x}$. The construction of $\widetilde{\varphi}_b$ exchanges the roles of the two populations.

We define the steering field $\boldsymbol{v}$ from the spatial gradient of the log-ratio of these two sums, holding the bracketed coefficients fixed at their values computed at $\boldsymbol{x}$ during differentiation:
\begin{equation}
\displaystyle
\boldsymbol{v}(\boldsymbol{x})
=b\nablaS
\log\frac{\widetilde{\psi}_b(\boldsymbol{x})}
{\widetilde{\varphi}_b(\boldsymbol{x})}.
\label{eq:cobras-field}
\end{equation}

Expanding Eq.~\eqref{eq:cobras-field} gives a weighted average of tangent vectors toward desired samples minus a weighted average toward undesired samples, yielding attraction and repulsion, respectively (Figure~\ref{fig:overview}b). For $\boldsymbol{v}(\boldsymbol{x})\ne\boldsymbol{0}$, the normalized vector $\boldsymbol{v}(\boldsymbol{x})/\|\boldsymbol{v}(\boldsymbol{x})\|$ gives the steering direction. In practice, we compute these averages of tangent vectors using a kernel bandwidth selected separately for each population at the current activation. Appendix~\ref{apx:bandwidth} specifies this construction. Having defined the steering field, we next determine how strongly to apply it to each activation in Section~\ref{subsec:strength}.

\subsection{Adaptive steering strength}
\label{subsec:strength}

Activation steering should weaken when an activation already aligns with the desired behavior or when the contrastive data provide little support for the prompt. We therefore normalize $\boldsymbol{v}(\boldsymbol{x})$ and control the intervention size separately through two factors. The coefficient $s$ reduces the steering strength for activations already aligned with the desired behavior, while the gate $g$ attenuates or disables steering on prompts with little contrastive support.

\noindent\textbf{Alignment coefficient.}~To determine $s$, we measure alignment with a unit contrastive axis $\boldsymbol{\mu}$, obtained by normalizing the difference between the desired and undesired sample means. We write $\cos(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\mu})$ for their cosine similarity, with larger values indicating stronger alignment with the desired behavior.

Following~\citep{you2026spherical}, we use von Mises--Fisher (vMF) scores for the desired direction $\boldsymbol{\mu}$ and the undesired direction $-\boldsymbol{\mu}$. With concentration $\kappa>0$, the desired and undesired scores are $e^{\kappa\cos(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\mu})}$ and $e^{-\kappa\cos(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\mu})}$, respectively.  We divide the undesired score by the sum of both scores to obtain
\[
\displaystyle
s(\boldsymbol{x})
=
\frac{e^{-\kappa\cos(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\mu})}}
{e^{\kappa\cos(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\mu})}
+e^{-\kappa\cos(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\mu})}}
=
\frac{1}{1+e^{2\kappa\cos(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\mu})}}.
\]
When the desired score dominates, $s$ is smaller and the intervention weakens. The concentration $\kappa$ controls how sharply the strength changes with alignment.

\noindent\textbf{Support gate.}~Alignment with $\boldsymbol{\mu}$ alone does not establish proximity to the contrastive data. In particular, a prompt activation can align with the undesired direction and receive a large coefficient $s$ even when it has little support from the contrastive data. We therefore use the gate $g$ to attenuate steering on prompts for which contrastive samples provide little support.

To assess support at the prompt activation $\boldsymbol{x}$, we use the fitted endpoint potentials directly as weights in a kernel sum over each contrastive population. Using the kernel in Eq.~\eqref{eq:kernel} with support bandwidth $b_g>0$, we define
\begin{equation}
\displaystyle
\widehat{\varphi}_{b_g}(\boldsymbol{x})=\sum\nolimits_j \widehat{\varphi}_{0,j}K_{b_g}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{h}_j^-),
\qquad
\widehat{\psi}_{b_g}(\boldsymbol{x})=\sum\nolimits_i \widehat{\psi}_{1,i}K_{b_g}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{h}_i^+).
\label{eq:frozen-factors}
\end{equation}
Motivated by the product form of the bridge density in Eq.~\eqref{eq:product}, we define an unnormalized support score by multiplying the two kernel sums. For calibration, we use its negative logarithm:
\begin{equation}
\displaystyle
\hat{p}_{b_g}(\boldsymbol{x})
=\widehat{\varphi}_{b_g}(\boldsymbol{x})\widehat{\psi}_{b_g}(\boldsymbol{x}),
\qquad
u(\boldsymbol{x})=-\log\hat{p}_{b_g}(\boldsymbol{x}).
\label{eq:support-score}
\end{equation}
Larger values of $u$ indicate lower support. To assess whether the prompt activation $\boldsymbol{x}$ is out of distribution (OOD), we compare $u(\boldsymbol{x})$ with scores computed on a reference set $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{ref}}$ of in-distribution prompt activations held out from evaluation. Both reference and query activations are extracted from the last prompt token at the steering layer. For $n=|\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{ref}}|$, we define the reference percentile $r(\boldsymbol{x})\in[0,1]$ as the fraction of reference activations whose scores are lower than $u(\boldsymbol{x})$:
\[
\displaystyle
r(\boldsymbol{x})
=\frac{|\{\boldsymbol{z}\in\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{ref}}:u(\boldsymbol{z})<u(\boldsymbol{x})\}|}{n}.
\]
A reference percentile close to one indicates that $\boldsymbol{x}$ has lower contrastive support than most reference activations. We use a coverage parameter $p\in(0,1)$ to set the percentile above which steering is attenuated. We define the support gate as
\begin{equation}
\displaystyle
g(\boldsymbol{x})=\min\!\left\{1,\frac{1-r(\boldsymbol{x})}{1-p}\right\}.
\label{eq:gate}
\end{equation}
The gate leaves steering strength unchanged when $r(\boldsymbol{x})\le p$ and decreases linearly with $r(\boldsymbol{x})$ above $p$, disabling steering when $u(\boldsymbol{x})$ exceeds every reference score. Appendix~\ref{apx:ood-gate} examines this calibration and evaluates how well the scores distinguish in-distribution and OOD prompt activations.

\begin{remark}
The support construction also motivates a simplified, universal kNN gate that can be seamlessly integrated as an external module into existing steering methods. Using nearest-neighbor distances to the undesired contrastive samples, this gate scales each method's intervention without changing how that intervention is computed. Appendix~\ref{apx:knn} presents the gate, explains its connection to the support score, and evaluates it across steering methods.\end{remark}

\subsection{Applying the intervention during generation}
\label{subsec:procedure}

We now describe the inference procedure for \Cobras. To do so, we combine the steering field $\boldsymbol{v}$ with the alignment coefficient $s$ and support gate $g$ to steer activations during generation. For each token, let $\boldsymbol{x}_0\in\Sph$ denote its activation before steering. When processing the last prompt token, we compute $g_{\mathrm{prompt}}:=g(\boldsymbol{x}_0)$ and use this value for steering that activation and all subsequent token activations. For each token activation, we evaluate $s(\boldsymbol{x}_0)$ once and keep it fixed during steering. We use the initial angular separation $\theta_0=\arccos\!\bigl(\cos(\boldsymbol{x}_0,\boldsymbol{\mu})\bigr)$ between $\boldsymbol{x}_0$ and the contrastive axis $\boldsymbol{\mu}$ to scale the steering speed, with $s(\boldsymbol{x}_0)$ and $g_{\mathrm{prompt}}$ providing attenuation. Because the steering field depends on the current activation, we take $K$ successive steps, recomputing $\boldsymbol{v}(\boldsymbol{x}_k)$ at each step $k=0,\ldots,K-1$. For an integration horizon $T\ge0$, the geodesic Euler updates are
\begin{equation}
\displaystyle
\Delta=\frac{T}{K}\,s(\boldsymbol{x}_0)\,g_{\mathrm{prompt}}\,\theta_0,
\qquad
\boldsymbol{x}_{k+1}
=\exp_{\boldsymbol{x}_k}\!\left(
\Delta\frac{\boldsymbol{v}(\boldsymbol{x}_k)}{\|\boldsymbol{v}(\boldsymbol{x}_k)\|}
\right).
\label{eq:step}
\end{equation}
Each update follows a geodesic arc of length $\Delta$ in the current steering direction through the exponential map, keeping the activation on $\Sph$. Algorithm~\ref{alg:cobras} summarizes the procedure.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{\Cobras}
\label{alg:cobras}
\small
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Contrastive activations $\mathcal{D}^\pm\subset\Sph$, reference prompt activations $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{ref}}\subset\Sph$,
regularization strength $\varepsilon$, support bandwidth $b_g$,
integration horizon $T$, coverage $p$, steps $K$, concentration $\kappa$
\Statex \textbf{Offline fitting and calibration}
\State Form the reference weights $\widehat{Q}_{01,ji}$ by Eq.~\eqref{eq:discrete-reference}
\State Fit the endpoint potentials $\widehat{\varphi}_{0,j}$ and $\widehat{\psi}_{1,i}$ using Sinkhorn as described in Section~\ref{subsec:sb}
\State Compute the contrastive axis $\boldsymbol{\mu}$ as defined in Section~\ref{subsec:strength}
\State Store the sorted reference scores $u(\boldsymbol{z})$ for $\boldsymbol{z}\in\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{ref}}$ using Eq.~\eqref{eq:support-score}
\Statex \textbf{Online steering of $\boldsymbol{x}_0\in\Sph$, starting with the last prompt token}
\If{$\boldsymbol{x}_0$ is the last prompt token's activation}
    \State Compute $g_{\mathrm{prompt}}\gets g(\boldsymbol{x}_0)$ by Eq.~\eqref{eq:gate} and cache it for the continuation
\Else
    \State Reuse the cached $g_{\mathrm{prompt}}$ for the current prompt
\EndIf
\State Evaluate $s(\boldsymbol{x}_0)$ and compute the fixed step size $\Delta$ using Eq.~\eqref{eq:step}
\If{$\Delta=0$}
    \State \Return $\boldsymbol{x}_0$
\EndIf
\For{$k=0,\ldots,K-1$}
    \State Compute $\boldsymbol{v}_k\gets\boldsymbol{v}(\boldsymbol{x}_k)$ using the steering construction in Section~\ref{subsec:estimation}
    \If{$\|\boldsymbol{v}_k\|>0$}
        \State $\boldsymbol{x}_{k+1}\gets
        \exp_{\boldsymbol{x}_k}(\Delta\,\boldsymbol{v}_k/\|\boldsymbol{v}_k\|)$
    \Else
        \State $\boldsymbol{x}_{k+1}\gets\boldsymbol{x}_k$
    \EndIf
\EndFor
\State \Return $\boldsymbol{x}_K$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}
