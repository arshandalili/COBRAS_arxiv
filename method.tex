\section{Background}
\label{sec:bg}

\paragraph{Setup and notation.}
We fix a decoder-only transformer of residual width $d$ and a layer index $\ell$, and write $\boldsymbol{h}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$ for the layer-$\ell$ residual-stream activation at the last token position. Two populations of inputs are given, one exhibiting a desired behavior and one an undesired behavior, with activation distributions $\pplus$ and $\pminus$ observed through i.i.d.\ samples $\mathcal{D}^{+}=\{\boldsymbol{h}^{+}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N_{+}}\sim\pplus$ and $\mathcal{D}^{-}=\{\boldsymbol{h}^{-}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N_{-}}\sim\pminus$, whose empirical measures we denote $\pplushat$ and $\pminushat$. At inference, a steering map is applied to the layer-$\ell$ activation of each decoded token, beginning with the last prompt token, and $\hquery$ denotes the activation currently being steered. The map should move $\hquery$ toward regions of high density under $\pplus$ and low density under $\pminus$, and it modifies no other activation. Vectors and vector fields are set in bold.

\paragraph{Spherical geometry of activations.}
Residual-stream norms concentrate tightly across inputs at a fixed layer, both empirically~\citep{pham2024householder, you2026spherical} and as a consequence of the residual architecture~\citep{yang2023spectral, xie2026controlled}. Figure~\ref{fig:norm-concentration} shows this for the four models we study, for which the coefficient of variation of the layer-normalized last-token norm lies between $2.5\%$ and $7.3\%$, and Appendix~\ref{appendix:norm_concentration} reports the same measurement layer by layer and for four further architectures. We therefore model the state space as the sphere $\Sph=\{\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\lVert\boldsymbol{x}\rVert=R\}$ of radius $R$, the mean norm over $\mathcal{D}^{+}\cup\mathcal{D}^{-}$, and restore the original norm of $\hquery$ after steering, so that the intervention changes only the direction of the activation.

\paragraph{Operations on $\Sph$.}
We write $\Pi_{\boldsymbol{x}}=\boldsymbol{I}-R^{-2}\boldsymbol{x}\boldsymbol{x}^{\top}$ for the projector onto the tangent space at $\boldsymbol{x}$, $\nablaS f=\Pi_{\boldsymbol{x}}\nabla f$ for the Riemannian gradient, and $d_{\Sph}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})=R\arccos(R^{-2}\boldsymbol{x}^{\top}\boldsymbol{y})$ for the geodesic distance. The exponential map $\exp_{\boldsymbol{x}}(\boldsymbol{v})$ moves from $\boldsymbol{x}$ along the tangent vector $\boldsymbol{v}$ for a geodesic arc of length $\lVert\boldsymbol{v}\rVert$, and its inverse $\log_{\boldsymbol{x}}(\boldsymbol{y})$, defined for $\boldsymbol{y}\neq-\boldsymbol{x}$, is the tangent vector at $\boldsymbol{x}$ that points toward $\boldsymbol{y}$ with norm $d_{\Sph}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})$:
\begin{equation}
\exp_{\boldsymbol{x}}(\boldsymbol{v})
= \cos\!\left(\frac{\lVert\boldsymbol{v}\rVert}{R}\right)\boldsymbol{x}
+ R\sin\!\left(\frac{\lVert\boldsymbol{v}\rVert}{R}\right)\frac{\boldsymbol{v}}{\lVert\boldsymbol{v}\rVert},
\qquad
\log_{\boldsymbol{x}}(\boldsymbol{y}) = \frac{\theta}{\sin\theta}\,\Pi_{\boldsymbol{x}}\boldsymbol{y},
\quad \theta = \frac{d_{\Sph}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})}{R}.
\label{eq:expmap}
\end{equation}
The steering field derived below is a weighted sum of logarithm maps toward the contrastive samples, and each steering step is an exponential map. At zero tangent length or zero angular separation, the formulas use their continuous limits.

\section{LLM Steering as a Schr\"{o}dinger Bridge}
\label{sec:method}

In this section, we first cast steering as a transportation problem and then derive our method, \Cobras. We first derive the continuous bridge identities that motivate our construction (Section~\ref{subsec:sb}), then derive the direction, strength controls, and inference procedure (Sections~\ref{subsec:estimation}--\ref{subsec:procedure}). Section~\ref{subsec:reduction} establishes the connection to density-ratio steering, which is prior works implicitly optimized.

\subsection{Schr\"{o}dinger Bridge and transport objective}
\label{subsec:sb}

We formulate steering as transporting the undesired activation distribution $\pminus$ to the desired distribution $\pplus$ on $\Sph$. Let $P$ be a probability distribution over continuous activation trajectories, and let $P_t$ denote the distribution of activations at transport time $t\in[0,1]$ when trajectories are drawn from $P$. To choose among transports with these endpoints, we compare $P$ with a reference trajectory distribution $Q$. We take $Q$ to describe Brownian motion on $\Sph$ with diffusion scale $\sigma^2$ and a uniform initial distribution, providing random motion without a preferred direction on the sphere. The Schr\"{o}dinger bridge~\citep{schrodinger1932theorie} is the trajectory distribution that matches the two endpoints while minimizing the Kullback--Leibler (KL) divergence from $Q$,
\begin{equation}
P^\star=\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{P}\KL(P\,\Vert\,Q)
\qquad\text{s.t.}\qquad P_0=\pminus,\quad P_1=\pplus.
\label{eq:sb}
\end{equation}
Thus the endpoint constraints specify the desired population change, and relative entropy selects the evolution closest to the reference motion. The time variable $t\in[0,1]$ indexes this population transport independently of token positions in the generated sequence.

The solution is characterized by two positive Schr\"{o}dinger potentials, $\varphi(\boldsymbol{x},t)$ and $\psi(\boldsymbol{x},t)$, which propagate information from the undesired and desired endpoints, respectively~\citep{leonard2013survey}. Together they determine the bridge density through their product,
\begin{equation}
p_t(\boldsymbol{x})\propto\varphi(\boldsymbol{x},t)\psi(\boldsymbol{x},t).
\label{eq:product}
\end{equation}
Starting from $\pminus$, a deterministic flow can reproduce the bridge's activation distribution at every time $t$~\citep{song2020score}. Its direction follows the gradient of the log-ratio of the two potentials,
\begin{equation}
\frac{d\boldsymbol{x}_t}{dt}
=\frac{\sigma^2}{2}\nablaS\log
\frac{\psi(\boldsymbol{x}_t,t)}{\varphi(\boldsymbol{x}_t,t)}.
\label{eq:ldr}
\end{equation}
These identities, derived in Appendix~\ref{apx:static}, motivate a potential \emph{ratio} for the steering direction and a potential \emph{product} for support. Since the potentials describe the intermediate bridge rather than the endpoint densities, the connection to ordinary density-ratio steering requires the analysis of it in the limit, as done in Section~\ref{subsec:reduction}.

\subsection{Computing the direction from contrastive samples}
\label{subsec:estimation}

Motivated by the bridge formulation, we construct a steering direction from contrastive activations.

\paragraph{Transport plan estimation.}
Project $\mathcal D^\pm$ onto $\Sph$ and define the kernel
\begin{equation}
K_b(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})
=\exp\!\left[-\frac{d_{\Sph}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})^2}{2b}\right],
\qquad b>0.
\label{eq:kernel}
\end{equation}
The bandwidth $b$ sets the scale of this kernel, whose form is motivated by the leading short-time exponential behavior of the heat kernel~\citep{chen2023logarithmic}. Using it as a surrogate for the spherical Brownian transition density in a discrete entropic transport problem gives the plan
\begin{equation}
\pi^\star_{ji}=a_j^-a_i^+e^{-c_{ji}},
\qquad
c_{ji}=\frac{d_{\Sph}(\boldsymbol h_j^-,\boldsymbol h_i^+)^2}{2\varepsilon}.
\label{eq:plan}
\end{equation}
The entry $\pi^\star_{ji}$ assigns mass from undesired sample $j$ to desired sample $i$, with $\varepsilon$ controlling the cost--entropy tradeoff. The positive scalings $a_j^-,a_i^+$ enforce uniform row and column marginals and are fitted using Sinkhorn updates~\citep{cuturi2013sinkhorn}, as detailed in Appendix~\ref{apx:static}.

\paragraph{Transport weights and steering direction.}
For the current token activation $\boldsymbol{x}$ being steered, we weight each desired contrastive activation $\boldsymbol h_i^+$ by both its proximity to $\boldsymbol{x}$ and its transport connections to undesired contrastive activations $\boldsymbol h_j^-$ near $\boldsymbol{x}$. We encode these connections by
\begin{align}
m^+_{i,b}(\boldsymbol{x})
&=\sum_j a_j^-K_b(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol h_j^-)\,e^{-c_{ji}},
\label{eq:ext-psi}\\
m^-_{j,b}(\boldsymbol{x})
&=\sum_i a_i^+K_b(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol h_i^+)\,e^{-c_{ji}}.
\label{eq:ext-phi}
\end{align}
The coefficient $m^+_{i,b}(\boldsymbol{x})$ combines the proximity of undesired activations $\boldsymbol h_j^-$ to $\boldsymbol{x}$ with their transport costs to the desired activation $\boldsymbol h_i^+$, weighted by the fitted scalings $a_j^-$. Multiplying by $K_b(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol h_i^+)$ accounts for the desired activation's proximity, and normalization gives
\begin{equation}
\bar w_i^+(\boldsymbol{x})
=\frac{m^+_{i,b}(\boldsymbol{x})K_b(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol h_i^+)}
{\sum_{i'}m^+_{i',b}(\boldsymbol{x})K_b(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol h_{i'}^+)},
\label{eq:weights-pos}
\end{equation}
with $\bar w_j^-$ defined symmetrically. The steering field is
\begin{equation}
\boldsymbol v(\boldsymbol{x})
=\underbrace{\sum_i\bar w_i^+(\boldsymbol{x})\log_{\boldsymbol{x}}(\boldsymbol h_i^+)}_{\text{attraction}}
-\underbrace{\sum_j\bar w_j^-(\boldsymbol{x})\log_{\boldsymbol{x}}(\boldsymbol h_j^-)}_{\text{repulsion}}.
\label{eq:cobras-field}
\end{equation}
The field combines attraction toward desired activations $\boldsymbol h_i^+$ with repulsion from undesired activations $\boldsymbol h_j^-$, with weights recomputed at each steering step. Appendix~\ref{app:grad-psi} explains its relationship to the potential-ratio formulation. The implementation uses separate adaptive bandwidths for the two populations, as detailed in Appendix~\ref{apx:bandwidth}.

\subsection{Adaptive steering strength}
\label{subsec:strength}

We normalize $\boldsymbol v$ to separate the steering direction from the update size. The attenuation coefficient $s$ reduces this size as the activation aligns with the desired activations, while the support gate $g$ attenuates steering for prompts with low support under the contrastive data.

\paragraph{vMF attenuation.}
Following Spherical Steering~\citep{you2026spherical}, we use antipodal von Mises--Fisher (vMF) prototypes to measure alignment with the contrastive axis. Let $\bar{\boldsymbol h}^{\pm}$ be the means of the projected samples and define the desired prototype
\begin{equation}
\boldsymbol\mu
=\frac{\bar{\boldsymbol h}^+-\bar{\boldsymbol h}^-}
{\|\bar{\boldsymbol h}^+-\bar{\boldsymbol h}^-\|}.
\label{eq:mu}
\end{equation}
The prototypes $\boldsymbol\mu$ and $-\boldsymbol\mu$ have vMF scores proportional to $e^{\pm\kappa\langle\boldsymbol{x}/R,\boldsymbol\mu\rangle}$, with shared concentration $\kappa>0$. We use the normalized score of the undesired prototype as the strength coefficient,
\begin{equation}
s(\boldsymbol{x})
=\frac{e^{-\kappa\langle\boldsymbol{x}/R,\boldsymbol\mu\rangle}}
{e^{\kappa\langle\boldsymbol{x}/R,\boldsymbol\mu\rangle}+e^{-\kappa\langle\boldsymbol{x}/R,\boldsymbol\mu\rangle}}
=\left(1+e^{2\kappa\langle\boldsymbol{x}/R,\boldsymbol\mu\rangle}\right)^{-1}.
\label{eq:side}
\end{equation}
Thus $s$ decreases as the activation aligns with $\boldsymbol\mu$, reducing the intervention on activations already oriented toward the desired prototype. We apply this smooth score directly, with $\kappa$ controlling the sharpness of the attenuation.

\paragraph{Support estimation and gate calibration.}
Alignment with $\boldsymbol\mu$ alone does not establish proximity to the contrastive data. To limit steering on out-of-distribution (OOD) prompts, we therefore assess the proximity of a prompt activation $\boldsymbol{x}$ to each contrastive population using the weighted kernel sums
\begin{equation}
\widehat\varphi_b(\boldsymbol{x})=\sum_j a_j^-K_b(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol h_j^-),
\qquad
\widehat\psi_b(\boldsymbol{x})=\sum_i a_i^+K_b(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol h_i^+).
\label{eq:frozen-factors}
\end{equation}
Following the product form of the bridge density in Eq.~\eqref{eq:product}, we define an unnormalized support score at bandwidth $b_g$,
\begin{equation}
\hat p_{b_g}(\boldsymbol{x})
=\widehat\varphi_{b_g}(\boldsymbol{x})\widehat\psi_{b_g}(\boldsymbol{x}),
\qquad
u(\boldsymbol{x})=-\log\hat p_{b_g}(\boldsymbol{x}).
\label{eq:support-score}
\end{equation}
Larger values of $u$ indicate lower support.

We calibrate $u$ using a reference set $\mathcal D_{\mathrm{ref}}$ of in-distribution prompt activations held out from evaluation. Both reference and query activations are extracted from the last prompt token at the steering layer and projected onto $\Sph$. For $n=|\mathcal D_{\mathrm{ref}}|$, we rank a query prompt's score against the reference scores and convert its rank into the gate
\begin{equation}
r(\boldsymbol{x})
=\frac{|\{\boldsymbol z\in\mathcal D_{\mathrm{ref}}:u(\boldsymbol z)<u(\boldsymbol{x})\}|}{n},
\qquad
g(\boldsymbol{x})=\min\!\left\{1,\frac{1-r(\boldsymbol{x})}{1-p}\right\}.
\label{eq:gate}
\end{equation}
With coverage parameter $p\in(0,1)$, the gate leaves the steering strength unchanged up to the reference $p$-quantile and then decreases linearly with rank, reaching zero for scores above all reference scores. Appendix~\ref{apx:ood-gate} explains how $p$ controls in-distribution steering strength and evaluates how well the support scores separate OOD prompts.

A simpler distance gate, based on the $k$-th nearest undesired activation, can replace this score. As the bandwidth $b_g$ shrinks, $u$ approaches the sum of the squared nearest-neighbor distances to the two populations, so the distance gate is a one-sided relative of the product score. Because it needs only distances, it can also gate other steering methods. Appendix~\ref{apx:knn-gate} derives this limit, reports the distance gate attached to baseline methods, and explains why we calibrate the product score on prompt activations instead.

\subsection{Applying the intervention during generation}
\label{subsec:procedure}

We combine the steering field $\boldsymbol v$ with the attenuation coefficient $s$ and support gate $g$ to update activations during generation. We compute the support gate $g_q$ once from the last prompt token's activation and reuse it throughout generation. For each incoming token activation $\hquery$ at layer $\ell$, we project it onto $\Sph$ as $\boldsymbol{x}_0=R\hquery/\|\hquery\|$ and evaluate the attenuation coefficient $s(\boldsymbol{x}_0)$. We then apply $K$ geodesic Euler steps with steering strength $T\ge0$,
\begin{equation}
\Delta=T\,s(\boldsymbol{x}_0)\,g_q\,\theta_0\frac{R}{K},
\qquad
\theta_0=\arccos\langle\boldsymbol{x}_0/R,\boldsymbol\mu\rangle,
\qquad
\boldsymbol{x}_{k+1}
=\exp_{\boldsymbol{x}_k}\!\left(
\Delta\frac{\boldsymbol v(\boldsymbol{x}_k)}{\|\boldsymbol v(\boldsymbol{x}_k)\|}
\right).
\label{eq:step}
\end{equation}
At each step, we recompute the direction $\boldsymbol v(\boldsymbol{x}_k)$ while keeping $s(\boldsymbol{x}_0)$ and $g_q$ fixed. We skip steps with a zero field, while $g_q=0$ disables steering throughout generation. After the $K$ steps, we return $\|\hquery\|\boldsymbol{x}_K/R$ to restore the activation's original norm. Here $\theta_0R$ is the geodesic distance from $\boldsymbol{x}_0$ to the desired prototype $R\boldsymbol\mu$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:mu}, so the total path length on $\Sph$ is $T\,s(\boldsymbol{x}_0)\,g_q\,\theta_0R\le T\theta_0R$: the strength $T$ sets the largest fraction of that distance the activation can travel. Algorithm~\ref{alg:cobras} summarizes the procedure, with experimental settings given in Appendix~\ref{appendix:implementation_details}.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{\Cobras with adaptive bandwidths}
\label{alg:cobras}
\small
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require $\mathcal D^\pm$; reference prompts $\mathcal D_{\mathrm{ref}}$;
transport scale $\varepsilon$; support bandwidth $b_g$;
strength $T$; coverage $p$; steps $K$; concentration $\kappa$
\Statex \textbf{Offline: fit transport and calibrate support}
\State $R\gets\mathrm{mean}_{\boldsymbol h\in\mathcal D^+\cup\mathcal D^-}\|\boldsymbol h\|$;
project all contrastive activations onto $\Sph$
\State Form $c_{ji}$ using Eq.~\eqref{eq:plan}; fit $\log\boldsymbol a^\pm$
using the Sinkhorn updates in Eq.~\eqref{eq:sinkhorn}
\State Compute $\boldsymbol\mu$ using Eq.~\eqref{eq:mu}
\State Store the sorted scores $u(R\boldsymbol z/\|\boldsymbol z\|)$
for $\boldsymbol z\in\mathcal D_{\mathrm{ref}}$
\Statex \textbf{Online: edit the current token activation $\hquery$}
\State $\boldsymbol{x}_0\gets R\hquery/\|\hquery\|$
\label{alg:cobras:norm_equation}
\If{this is the last prompt token}
    \State Compute $g_q$ by Eq.~\eqref{eq:gate} and cache it for the continuation
\EndIf
\State $s\gets s(\boldsymbol{x}_0)$;\quad
$\theta_0\gets\arccos\langle\boldsymbol{x}_0/R,\boldsymbol\mu\rangle$;\quad
$\Delta\gets Tsg_q\theta_0R/K$
\If{$\Delta=0$}
    \State \Return $\hquery$
\EndIf
\For{$k=0,\ldots,K-1$}
    \State Compute $b_\pm(\boldsymbol{x}_k)$ by Eq.~\eqref{eq:adaptive-bandwidth}
    \State $L_i^+\gets-d_{\Sph}(\boldsymbol{x}_k,\boldsymbol h_i^+)^2/(2b_+)$;
    $L_j^-\gets-d_{\Sph}(\boldsymbol{x}_k,\boldsymbol h_j^-)^2/(2b_-)$
    \State $M_i^+\gets\operatorname{logsumexp}_j[\log a_j^-+L_j^--c_{ji}]$;
    $M_j^-\gets\operatorname{logsumexp}_i[\log a_i^++L_i^+-c_{ji}]$
    \State $\bar w_i^+\gets\mathrm{softmax}_i(M_i^++L_i^+)$;
    $\bar w_j^-\gets\mathrm{softmax}_j(M_j^-+L_j^-)$
    \State Form $\boldsymbol v_k$ from Eq.~\eqref{eq:cobras-field}
    \If{$\|\boldsymbol v_k\|>0$}
        \State $\boldsymbol{x}_{k+1}\gets
        \exp_{\boldsymbol{x}_k}(\Delta\,\boldsymbol v_k/\|\boldsymbol v_k\|)$
    \Else
        \State $\boldsymbol{x}_{k+1}\gets\boldsymbol{x}_k$
    \EndIf
\EndFor
\State \Return $\|\hquery\|\boldsymbol{x}_K/R$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Connection to density-ratio steering}
\label{subsec:reduction}

Log-density-ratio steering favors regions where desired activations have higher density relative to undesired activations, using the direction
\begin{equation}
\boldsymbol v(\boldsymbol{x})\propto \nabla\log\frac{\pplus(\boldsymbol{x})}{\pminus(\boldsymbol{x})}.
\label{eq:ldr-ideal}
\end{equation}
For Gaussian models with identity covariance, this gradient gives the mean difference used by CAA~\citep{rimsky2024steering}. ODESteer estimates the log-ratio directly and follows its gradient~\citep{zhaoodesteer}, with further baseline interpretations in Appendix~\ref{apx:ldr-formalize}.

With the contrastive activations and a shared evaluation bandwidth $b>0$ fixed, taking $\varepsilon\to\infty$ yields $c_{ji}\to0$ and $e^{-c_{ji}}\to1$ while leaving the proximity kernels $K_b$ unchanged. In this limit, the coefficients $m^+_{i,b}$ no longer vary across desired activations, and likewise for $m^-_{j,b}$ across undesired activations. They therefore cancel during weight normalization, leaving weights determined only by proximity to $\boldsymbol{x}$.

The resulting field follows the log-ratio gradient of kernel estimates summarizing the two contrastive populations,
\begin{equation}
\hat p_b^\pm(\boldsymbol{x})=\frac{1}{N_\pm}\sum_k K_b(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol h_k^\pm),
\qquad
\boldsymbol v_b^{\mathrm{kde}}(\boldsymbol{x})
=b\nablaS\log\frac{\hat p_b^+(\boldsymbol{x})}{\hat p_b^-(\boldsymbol{x})}.
\label{eq:ldr-kde}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Reduction to the kernel density-ratio field]
\label{prop:ldr-limit}
Fix the contrastive activations on $\Sph$ and a shared bandwidth $b>0$. As $\varepsilon\to\infty$, the unnormalized field $\boldsymbol v$ converges to $\boldsymbol v_b^{\mathrm{kde}}$ at rate $O(1/\varepsilon)$, uniformly away from the contrastive activations' antipodes.
\end{proposition}

Proposition~\ref{prop:ldr-limit} shows that, at a shared fixed bandwidth, the \Cobras field converges to a kernel density-ratio field on the sphere as $\varepsilon\to\infty$. This recovers the steering principle represented by Gaussian densities in the interpretation of CAA and by a directly fitted log-ratio in ODESteer. At finite $\varepsilon$, transport connections reweight the attraction and repulsion contributions of individual contrastive activations. The proof is in Appendix~\ref{apx:proof-ldr-limit}.
