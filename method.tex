\section{Background}
\label{sec:bg}

\paragraph{Setup and notation.}
We fix a decoder-only transformer of residual width $d$ and a layer index $\ell$, and write $\boldsymbol{h}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$ for the layer-$\ell$ residual-stream activation at the last token position. Two populations of inputs are given, one exhibiting a desired behavior and one an undesired behavior, with activation distributions $\pplus$ and $\pminus$ observed through i.i.d.\ samples $\mathcal{D}^{+}=\{\boldsymbol{h}^{+}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N_{+}}\sim\pplus$ and $\mathcal{D}^{-}=\{\boldsymbol{h}^{-}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N_{-}}\sim\pminus$, whose empirical measures we denote $\pplushat$ and $\pminushat$. At inference, a steering map is applied to the layer-$\ell$ activation of each decoded token, beginning with the last prompt token, and $\hquery$ denotes the activation currently being steered. The map should move $\hquery$ toward regions of high density under $\pplus$ and low density under $\pminus$, and it modifies no other activation. Vectors and vector fields are set in bold.

\paragraph{Spherical geometry of activations.}
Residual-stream norms concentrate tightly across inputs at a fixed layer, both empirically~\citep{pham2024householder, you2026spherical} and as a consequence of the residual architecture~\citep{yang2023spectral, xie2026controlled}. Figure~\ref{fig:norm-concentration} shows this for the four models we study, for which the coefficient of variation of the layer-normalized last-token norm lies between $2.5\%$ and $7.3\%$, and Appendix~\ref{appendix:norm_concentration} reports the same measurement layer by layer and for four further architectures. We therefore model the state space as the sphere $\Sph=\{\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\lVert\boldsymbol{x}\rVert=R\}$ of radius $R$, the mean norm over $\mathcal{D}^{+}\cup\mathcal{D}^{-}$, and restore the original norm of $\hquery$ after steering, so that the intervention changes only the direction of the activation.

\paragraph{Operations on $\Sph$.}
We write $\Pi_{\boldsymbol{x}}=\boldsymbol{I}-R^{-2}\boldsymbol{x}\boldsymbol{x}^{\top}$ for the projector onto the tangent space at $\boldsymbol{x}$, $\nablaS f=\Pi_{\boldsymbol{x}}\nabla f$ for the Riemannian gradient, and $d_{\Sph}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})=R\arccos(R^{-2}\boldsymbol{x}^{\top}\boldsymbol{y})$ for the geodesic distance. The exponential map $\exp_{\boldsymbol{x}}(\boldsymbol{v})$ moves from $\boldsymbol{x}$ along the tangent vector $\boldsymbol{v}$ for a geodesic arc of length $\lVert\boldsymbol{v}\rVert$, and its inverse $\log_{\boldsymbol{x}}(\boldsymbol{y})$, defined for $\boldsymbol{y}\neq-\boldsymbol{x}$, is the tangent vector at $\boldsymbol{x}$ that points toward $\boldsymbol{y}$ with norm $d_{\Sph}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})$:
\begin{equation}
\exp_{\boldsymbol{x}}(\boldsymbol{v})
= \cos\!\left(\frac{\lVert\boldsymbol{v}\rVert}{R}\right)\boldsymbol{x}
+ R\sin\!\left(\frac{\lVert\boldsymbol{v}\rVert}{R}\right)\frac{\boldsymbol{v}}{\lVert\boldsymbol{v}\rVert},
\qquad
\log_{\boldsymbol{x}}(\boldsymbol{y}) = \frac{\theta}{\sin\theta}\,\Pi_{\boldsymbol{x}}\boldsymbol{y},
\quad \theta = \frac{d_{\Sph}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})}{R}.
\label{eq:expmap}
\end{equation}
The steering field derived below is a weighted sum of logarithm maps toward the contrastive samples, and each steering step is an exponential map. At zero tangent length or zero angular separation, the formulas use their continuous limits.

\section{LLM Steering as a Schr\"{o}dinger Bridge}
\label{sec:method}

In this section, we first cast steering as a distribution transportation problem and then derive our method. We first derive the continuous bridge identities that motivate our construction (Section~\ref{subsec:sb}), then derive the direction, strength controls, and inference procedure (Sections~\ref{subsec:estimation}--\ref{subsec:procedure}). Section~\ref{subsec:reduction} establishes the connection to density-ratio steering, which is implicitly optimized in prior work.

\subsection{Schr\"{o}dinger Bridge and transport objective}
\label{subsec:sb}

We formulate steering as transporting the undesired activation distribution $\pminus$ to the desired distribution $\pplus$ on $\Sph$. Let $P$ be a probability distribution over continuous activation trajectories, and let $P_t$ denote the distribution of activations at transport time $t\in[0,1]$ when trajectories are drawn from $P$. To choose among transports with these endpoints, we compare $P$ with a reference trajectory distribution $Q$. We take $Q$ to be the Brownian motion on $\Sph$ with diffusion scale $\sigma^2$ and a uniform initial distribution, providing random motion on the sphere without a preferred direction. The Schr\"{o}dinger bridge (SB) is the trajectory distribution that matches the two endpoints while minimizing the Kullback--Leibler (KL) divergence from $Q$~\citep{schrodinger1932theorie, de2021diffusion},
\begin{equation}
P^\star=\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{P}\KL(P\,\Vert\,Q)
\qquad\text{s.t.}\qquad P_0=\pminus,\quad P_1=\pplus.
\label{eq:sb}
\end{equation}
Thus the endpoint constraints specify the desired population change, and relative entropy selects the evolution closest to the reference motion. The time variable $t\in[0,1]$ indexes this population transport independently of token positions in the generated sequence.

\textcolor{blue}{The trajectory optimization in Eq.~\eqref{eq:sb} reduces to finding an endpoint coupling, the joint distribution of starting and ending activations. For a fixed coupling, the KL divergence is minimized by using the reference Brownian motion conditioned on each pair of endpoints. We use the resulting endpoint problem to motivate the discrete transport formulation in Section~\ref{subsec:estimation}.}

The solution to SB is characterized by two positive Schr\"{o}dinger potentials, $\varphi(\boldsymbol{x},t)$ and $\psi(\boldsymbol{x},t)$, which propagate information from the undesired forward and desired endpoints backward, respectively~\citep{leonard2013survey}. Together they determine the bridge density through their product,
\begin{equation}
p_t(\boldsymbol{x})\propto\varphi(\boldsymbol{x},t)\psi(\boldsymbol{x},t).
\label{eq:product}
\end{equation}
\textcolor{blue}{Starting from $\pminus$, a deterministic flow can reproduce the bridge's activation distribution $P_t$ at every time $t$~\citep{song2020score}. The flow follows the gradient of the log-ratio of the two potentials,}
\begin{equation}
\frac{\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}_t}{\mathrm{d}t}
=\frac{\sigma^2}{2}\nablaS\log
\frac{\psi(\boldsymbol{x}_t,t)}{\varphi(\boldsymbol{x}_t,t)}.
\label{eq:ldr}
\end{equation}
These identities in Eqs.~\eqref{eq:product} and \eqref{eq:ldr} motivate a potential \emph{ratio} for the steering direction and a potential \emph{product} for determining whether the obtained transport plan has support for the given activation at inference or not. \textcolor{blue}{Appendix~\ref{apx:static} gives the assumptions and derivation.}

\subsection{Computing the direction from contrastive samples}
\label{subsec:estimation}

Motivated by the bridge formulation, we construct a steering direction from contrastive activations.

\paragraph{Transport plan estimation.}
Project $\mathcal{D}^\pm$ onto $\Sph$ and define the kernel
\begin{equation}
K_b(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})
=\exp\!\left[-\frac{d_{\Sph}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})^2}{2b}\right],
\qquad b>0.
\label{eq:kernel}
\end{equation}
The bandwidth $b$ sets the scale of this kernel, whose form is motivated by the leading short-time exponential behavior of the heat kernel~\citep{chen2023logarithmic}. \textcolor{blue}{With entropy regularization strength $\varepsilon>0$, we use $K_\varepsilon$ as a surrogate for the spherical Brownian transition density and solve the discrete entropic transport problem}
\begin{equation}
\textcolor{blue}{\displaystyle
\pi^\star=\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{\pi\in\mathcal{U}}
\sum_{j,i}\pi_{ji}c_{ji}+\sum_{j,i}\pi_{ji}\log\pi_{ji},
\qquad
c_{ji}=\frac{d_{\Sph}(\boldsymbol{h}_j^-,\boldsymbol{h}_i^+)^2}{2\varepsilon}.
}
\label{eq:e-ot}
\end{equation}
\textcolor{blue}{Here $\mathcal{U}$ is the set of nonnegative couplings with uniform marginals, $\sum_i\pi_{ji}=1/N_-$ and $\sum_j\pi_{ji}=1/N_+$. The cost term favors transport between nearby activations, while the entropy term encourages spreading mass across sample pairs. The optimal plan factorizes as}
\begin{equation}
\pi^\star_{ji}=a_j^-a_i^+e^{-c_{ji}}.
\label{eq:plan}
\end{equation}
The entry $\pi^\star_{ji}$ assigns mass from undesired sample $j$ to desired sample $i$, with $\varepsilon$ controlling the cost--entropy tradeoff. The positive scalings $a_j^-,a_i^+$ enforce the row and column marginals and are fitted using Sinkhorn updates~\citep{cuturi2013sinkhorn}, as detailed in Appendix~\ref{apx:static}.

\textcolor{blue}{We fit the transport plan once and keep the costs $c_{ji}$ and scalings $a_j^-,a_i^+$ fixed during generation. Steering a new token activation then requires combining the fitted connections with the activation's proximity to the contrastive samples.}
%For the current token activation $\boldsymbol{x}$ being steered
% Target activations $\boldsymbol{h}_i^+$ coupled via transport mappings to negative instances near $\boldsymbol{x}$ provide optimal contrastive resolution. Weighting $\boldsymbol{h}_i^+$ proportionally to these couplings accelerates boundary traversal and prevents steering vectors from drifting toward adjacent unintended regions
\paragraph{\textcolor{blue}{Steering direction from the transport plan.}}
To steer the current token activation $\boldsymbol{x}$, we weight each desired contrastive activation $\boldsymbol{h}_i^+$ by both its proximity to $\boldsymbol{x}$ and its transport connections to undesired contrastive activations $\boldsymbol{h}_j^-$ near $\boldsymbol{x}$. We encode these connections by
\begin{align}
m^+_{i,b}(\boldsymbol{x})
&=\sum_j a_j^-K_b(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{h}_j^-)\,e^{-c_{ji}},
\label{eq:ext-psi}\\
m^-_{j,b}(\boldsymbol{x})
&=\sum_i a_i^+K_b(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{h}_i^+)\,e^{-c_{ji}}.
\label{eq:ext-phi}
\end{align}
The coefficient $m^+_{i,b}(\boldsymbol{x})$ combines the proximity of undesired activations $\boldsymbol{h}_j^-$ to $\boldsymbol{x}$ with their transport costs to the desired activation $\boldsymbol{h}_i^+$, weighted by the fitted scalings $a_j^-$. Multiplying by $K_b(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{h}_i^+)$ accounts for the desired activation's proximity, and normalization gives
\begin{equation}
\bar{w}_i^+(\boldsymbol{x})
=\frac{m^+_{i,b}(\boldsymbol{x})K_b(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{h}_i^+)}
{\sum_{i'}m^+_{i',b}(\boldsymbol{x})K_b(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{h}_{i'}^+)},
\label{eq:weights-pos}
\end{equation}
with $\bar{w}_j^-$ defined symmetrically. The steering field is
\begin{equation}
\boldsymbol{v}(\boldsymbol{x})
=\underbrace{\sum_i\bar{w}_i^+(\boldsymbol{x})\log_{\boldsymbol{x}}(\boldsymbol{h}_i^+)}_{\text{attraction}}
-\underbrace{\sum_j\bar{w}_j^-(\boldsymbol{x})\log_{\boldsymbol{x}}(\boldsymbol{h}_j^-)}_{\text{repulsion}}.
\label{eq:cobras-field}
\end{equation}
The field combines attraction toward desired activations $\boldsymbol{h}_i^+$ with repulsion from undesired activations $\boldsymbol{h}_j^-$, with weights recomputed at each steering step.

\textcolor{blue}{To relate the field in Eq.~\eqref{eq:cobras-field} to the log-ratio direction in Eq.~\eqref{eq:ldr}, consider a shared fixed bandwidth $b$. Holding the coefficients $m^+_{i,b}$ and $m^-_{j,b}$ at their current values during differentiation gives $\boldsymbol{v}(\boldsymbol{x})$ as $b$ times the Riemannian gradient of the log-ratio of the weighted kernel estimates for the desired and undesired populations. The field is therefore an ascent direction for the local log-ratio defined by the current coefficients. Appendix~\ref{app:grad-psi} provides the derivation and the additional terms from differentiating through the coefficients. During inference, we recompute the coefficients and adapt the bandwidth for each population at every step (Appendix~\ref{apx:bandwidth}). Reweighting the contrastive samples as the activation moves lets the steering direction adapt along the trajectory while reusing the fitted transport connections.}

\subsection{Adaptive steering strength}
\label{subsec:strength}

\textcolor{blue}{Steering should weaken when an activation already aligns with the desired behavior or when the contrastive data provide little support for the prompt. We therefore normalize $\boldsymbol{v}$ and control the update size separately through two factors. The vMF attenuation coefficient $s$ reduces the update as the activation aligns with the desired direction, while the support gate $g$ suppresses steering on prompts with low contrastive support.}

\paragraph{\textcolor{blue}{vMF strength attenuation.}}
\textcolor{blue}{Let $\bar{\boldsymbol{h}}^{\pm}$ denote the means of the projected samples and define the contrastive axis \mbox{$\boldsymbol{\mu}=(\bar{\boldsymbol{h}}^+-\bar{\boldsymbol{h}}^-)/\|\bar{\boldsymbol{h}}^+-\bar{\boldsymbol{h}}^-\|$}. Following Spherical Steering~\citep{you2026spherical}, we evaluate desired and undesired von Mises--Fisher (vMF) scores at $\boldsymbol{x}/R$ with mean directions $\boldsymbol{\mu}$ and $-\boldsymbol{\mu}$, respectively, and shared concentration $\kappa>0$. Dividing the undesired score by the sum of the two scores gives the attenuation coefficient $s(\boldsymbol{x})=\left(1+e^{2\kappa\langle\boldsymbol{x}/R,\boldsymbol{\mu}\rangle}\right)^{-1}$. The concentration $\kappa$ controls how sharply $s$ changes with alignment. Closer alignment with $\boldsymbol{\mu}$ lowers $s$, reducing the intervention on activations already oriented toward the desired direction.}

\paragraph{\textcolor{blue}{Support scoring and gate calibration.}}
Alignment with $\boldsymbol{\mu}$ alone does not establish proximity to the contrastive data. To limit steering on out-of-distribution (OOD) prompts, we therefore assess the proximity of a prompt activation $\boldsymbol{x}$ to each contrastive population using the \textcolor{blue}{weighted kernel estimates}
\begin{equation}
\widehat{\varphi}_b(\boldsymbol{x})=\sum_j a_j^-K_b(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{h}_j^-),
\qquad
\widehat{\psi}_b(\boldsymbol{x})=\sum_i a_i^+K_b(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{h}_i^+).
\label{eq:frozen-factors}
\end{equation}
\textcolor{blue}{The ratio $\widehat{\psi}_b(\boldsymbol{x})/\widehat{\varphi}_b(\boldsymbol{x})$ can be large even when both fitted factors are small, so relative preference alone does not establish proximity to the contrastive data. Multiplying the factors retains information about their joint magnitude.} Following the product form of the bridge density in Eq.~\eqref{eq:product}, \textcolor{blue}{we use the fitted factors to define} an unnormalized support score at bandwidth $b_g$,
\begin{equation}
\hat{p}_{b_g}(\boldsymbol{x})
=\widehat{\varphi}_{b_g}(\boldsymbol{x})\widehat{\psi}_{b_g}(\boldsymbol{x}),
\qquad
u(\boldsymbol{x})=-\log\hat{p}_{b_g}(\boldsymbol{x}).
\label{eq:support-score}
\end{equation}
\textcolor{blue}{The support score uses the same fitted transport scalings $a_j^-,a_i^+$ as the steering weights.} Larger values of $u$ indicate lower support.

We calibrate $u$ using a reference set $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{ref}}$ of in-distribution prompt activations held out from evaluation. Both reference and query activations are extracted from the last prompt token at the steering layer and projected onto $\Sph$. For $n=|\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{ref}}|$, we rank a query prompt's score against the reference scores and convert its rank into the gate
\begin{equation}
r(\boldsymbol{x})
=\frac{|\{\boldsymbol{z}\in\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{ref}}:u(\boldsymbol{z})<u(\boldsymbol{x})\}|}{n},
\qquad
g(\boldsymbol{x})=\min\!\left\{1,\frac{1-r(\boldsymbol{x})}{1-p}\right\}.
\label{eq:gate}
\end{equation}
With coverage parameter $p\in(0,1)$, the gate leaves the steering strength unchanged up to the reference $p$-quantile and then decreases linearly with rank, reaching zero for scores above all reference scores. Appendix~\ref{apx:ood-gate} explains how $p$ controls in-distribution steering strength and evaluates how well the support scores separate OOD prompts.

\begin{remark}
\textcolor{blue}{We also implement a kNN distance gate that can be attached as an external module to other steering methods. The gate depends only on the query and contrastive activations and scales each method's own intervention. Appendix~\ref{apx:knn} presents the gate, its connection to the support score, and evaluations across steering methods.}
\end{remark}

\textcolor{red}{Building upon our proposed steering framework, we demonstrate that a simplified version of our algorithm yields a universal kNN distance gate that can be seamlessly integrated as an external module into existing methods. This lightweight gate relies solely on the query and contrastive activations to dynamically scale each baseline method's intervention. Appendix~\ref{apx:knn} details the formulation of the gate, its  connection to the support score, and comprehensive  evaluations across steering methods.}
\subsection{Applying the intervention during generation}
\label{subsec:procedure}

We combine the steering field $\boldsymbol{v}$ with the attenuation coefficient $s$ and support gate $g$ to update activations during generation. We compute the support gate $g_q$ once from the last prompt token's activation and reuse it throughout generation. For each incoming token activation $\hquery$ at layer $\ell$, we project it onto $\Sph$ as $\boldsymbol{x}_0=R\hquery/\|\hquery\|$ and evaluate the attenuation coefficient $s(\boldsymbol{x}_0)$. We then apply $K$ geodesic Euler steps with steering strength $T\ge0$,
\begin{equation}
\Delta=T\,s(\boldsymbol{x}_0)\,g_q\,\theta_0\frac{R}{K},
\qquad
\theta_0=\arccos\langle\boldsymbol{x}_0/R,\boldsymbol{\mu}\rangle,
\qquad
\boldsymbol{x}_{k+1}
=\exp_{\boldsymbol{x}_k}\!\left(
\Delta\frac{\boldsymbol{v}(\boldsymbol{x}_k)}{\|\boldsymbol{v}(\boldsymbol{x}_k)\|}
\right).
\label{eq:step}
\end{equation}
At each step, we recompute the direction $\boldsymbol{v}(\boldsymbol{x}_k)$ while keeping $s(\boldsymbol{x}_0)$ and $g_q$ fixed. After the $K$ steps, we return $\|\hquery\|\boldsymbol{x}_K/R$ to restore the activation's original norm. \textcolor{blue}{Here $\theta_0R$ is the geodesic distance from $\boldsymbol{x}_0$ to the point $R\boldsymbol{\mu}$ on the sphere, so the total path length on $\Sph$ is at most $T\,s(\boldsymbol{x}_0)\,g_q\,\theta_0R\le T\theta_0R$: the strength $T$ sets the largest fraction of that distance the activation can transport.} Algorithm~\ref{alg:cobras} summarizes the procedure, with experimental settings given in Appendix~\ref{appendix:implementation_details}.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{\Cobras}
\label{alg:cobras}
\small
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Contrastive activations $\mathcal{D}^\pm$, reference prompt activations $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{ref}}$,
regularization strength $\varepsilon$, support bandwidth $b_g$,
strength $T$, coverage $p$, steps $K$, concentration $\kappa$
\Statex \textbf{Offline fitting and calibration}
\State Set $R$ to the mean norm over $\mathcal{D}^+\cup\mathcal{D}^-$ and project all contrastive and reference activations onto $\Sph$
\State Form costs $c_{ji}$ by Eq.~\eqref{eq:e-ot} and fit the scalings $a_j^-,a_i^+$ using Eq.~\eqref{eq:sinkhorn}
\State Compute the contrastive axis $\boldsymbol{\mu}$ as defined in Section~\ref{subsec:strength}
\State Store the sorted reference scores $u(\boldsymbol{z})$ for $\boldsymbol{z}\in\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{ref}}$ using Eq.~\eqref{eq:support-score}
\Statex \textbf{Online steering of $\hquery$ at layer $\ell$, starting with the last prompt token}
\State $\boldsymbol{x}_0\gets R\hquery/\|\hquery\|$
\label{alg:cobras:norm_equation}
\If{$\hquery$ is the last prompt token's activation}
    \State Compute $g_q\gets g(\boldsymbol{x}_0)$ by Eq.~\eqref{eq:gate} and cache it for the continuation
\Else
    \State Reuse the cached $g_q$ for the current prompt
\EndIf
\State Evaluate $s(\boldsymbol{x}_0)$ and compute the fixed step size $\Delta$ using Eq.~\eqref{eq:step}
\If{$\Delta=0$}
    \State \Return $\hquery$
\EndIf
\For{$k=0,\ldots,K-1$}
    \State Select the bandwidths $b_\pm(\boldsymbol{x}_k)$ by Eq.~\eqref{eq:adaptive-bandwidth}
    \State Compute the conditional coefficients and normalized weights in Eqs.~\eqref{eq:ext-psi}--\eqref{eq:weights-pos},
    using $b_+$ for kernels centered on $\mathcal{D}^+$ and $b_-$ for kernels centered on $\mathcal{D}^-$
    \State $\boldsymbol{v}_k\gets\boldsymbol{v}(\boldsymbol{x}_k)$ using Eq.~\eqref{eq:cobras-field}
    \If{$\|\boldsymbol{v}_k\|>0$}
        \State $\boldsymbol{x}_{k+1}\gets
        \exp_{\boldsymbol{x}_k}(\Delta\,\boldsymbol{v}_k/\|\boldsymbol{v}_k\|)$
    \Else
        \State $\boldsymbol{x}_{k+1}\gets\boldsymbol{x}_k$
    \EndIf
\EndFor
\State \Return $\|\hquery\|\boldsymbol{x}_K/R$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Connection to density-ratio steering}
\label{subsec:reduction}

We now show how the transport-based field recovers the density-ratio steering principle used to interpret prior methods. Log-density-ratio steering follows $\nabla\log[\pplus(\boldsymbol{x})/\pminus(\boldsymbol{x})]$, favoring regions where desired activations have higher density relative to undesired activations. For Gaussian models with identity covariance, the gradient gives the mean difference used by CAA~\citep{rimsky2024steering}. ODESteer estimates the log-ratio directly and follows its gradient~\citep{zhaoodesteer}. Appendix~\ref{apx:ldr-formalize} gives further baseline interpretations.

To isolate the contribution of the transport connections, fix the contrastive activations and a shared evaluation bandwidth $b>0$, then increase the entropy regularization strength $\varepsilon$. As $\varepsilon\to\infty$, the costs $c_{ji}$ vanish and $e^{-c_{ji}}\to1$, while the proximity kernels $K_b$ remain unchanged. The coefficients $m^+_{i,b}$ then become independent of the desired-sample index $i$, and likewise for $m^-_{j,b}$ across undesired samples. The common coefficients cancel during weight normalization, leaving weights determined only by proximity to $\boldsymbol{x}$.
The corresponding kernel density-ratio field is \mbox{$\boldsymbol{v}_b^{\mathrm{kde}}(\boldsymbol{x})=b\nablaS\log[\hat{p}_b^+(\boldsymbol{x})/\hat{p}_b^-(\boldsymbol{x})]$}, where \mbox{$\hat{p}_b^\pm(\boldsymbol{x})=N_\pm^{-1}\sum_k K_b(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{h}_k^\pm)$} estimates each population.

\begin{proposition}[Reduction to the kernel density-ratio field]
\label{prop:ldr-limit}
Fix the contrastive activations on $\Sph$ and a shared bandwidth $b>0$. As $\varepsilon\to\infty$, the unnormalized field $\boldsymbol{v}$ converges to $\boldsymbol{v}_b^{\mathrm{kde}}$ at rate $O(1/\varepsilon)$, uniformly away from the contrastive activations' antipodes.
\end{proposition}

\textcolor{blue}{Proposition~\ref{prop:ldr-limit} establishes a limiting relationship at a shared fixed bandwidth. Appendix~\ref{apx:proof-ldr-limit} gives the proof and discusses the separate sampling limit needed to recover the population log-density-ratio gradient. The reduction identifies what transport contributes beyond density-ratio steering. At finite $\varepsilon$, each sample's influence depends on both its proximity to the query and its transport connections to the opposite population.}
