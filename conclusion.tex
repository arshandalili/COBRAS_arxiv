\vspace{-1mm}\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We introduced \Cobras, a framework for activation steering based on Schr\"{o}dinger bridges that connects transport-based steering to log-density-ratio objectives. The fitted transport supplies query-dependent steering directions and support information for controlling when to intervene. Experiments across four models and three alignment objectives show strong in-distribution performance, while evaluations under distribution shift demonstrate the value of support gating for retaining OOD capabilities.

The framework has both theoretical and practical limitations. The density-ratio reduction assumes a shared fixed bandwidth and vanishing transport costs, and does not establish optimality of the practical adaptive steering rule. Pairwise distance computations and Sinkhorn updates incur costs proportional to $N_+N_-$, limiting scalability to large contrastive sets, while the assumed spherical geometry may not hold across all architectures or layers. These limitations motivate further theoretical analysis of the adaptive steering rule, hierarchical transport solvers, and extensions to learned or more general Riemannian geometries.
