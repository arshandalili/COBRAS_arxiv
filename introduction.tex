\section{Introduction}
Activation steering has emerged as a compelling alternative to fine-tuning for shaping the behavior of large language models (LLMs) at inference time \citep{rimsky2024steering, li2023inference, wang2025truthflow}. Its empirical success is often motivated by the linear representation hypothesis \citep{park2023linear, alain2016understanding}, where many high-level properties encoded by an LLM, such as sentiment, truthfulness, or harmfulness, appear to correspond to approximately linear directions in activation space. Under this view, changing a model's behavior can be achieved by moving its internal activations along directions associated with the target property at inference time. A standard approach is activation addition, which modifies the original activations by adding either a fixed steering vector or one that depends on the current activation.

This perspective has inspired a growing body of work on inference-time control through activation interventions. Early methods construct a single steering direction from contrastive examples and apply it uniformly across queries~\citep{rimsky2024steering}. More recent approaches use continuous steering fields~\citep{zhaoodesteer, wang2025truthflow}, transport maps between activation distributions~\citep{rodriguez2025controlling}, or norm-preserving rotations~\citep{you2026spherical, pham2024householder}. Specifically, ODESteer~\citep{zhaoodesteer} and SphericalSteer~\citep{you2026spherical} adapt their updates to the current activation.

The remaining challenge is to characterize a population-level steering objective, which prior baselines implicitly optimize, along with a criterion for applying the resulting field to new prompts. A query-dependent direction alone does not indicate whether the contrastive data support an intervention on an out-of-distribution (OOD) input. Our experiments show that several strong baselines improve TruthfulQA~\citep{lin2021truthfulqa} at the cost of substantial MMLU~\citep{hendrycks2020measuring} and GSM8K~\citep{cobbe2021gsm8k} degradation. To this end, we introduce \Cobras, which formulates steering through a Schr\"{o}dinger Bridge (SB) on the activation hypersphere. The continuous formulation yields a log-density-ratio field, while the fitted transport supplies both query-dependent sample weights for steering and a support score for controlling OOD interventions.

Overall, our contributions are: (i) we formulate activation transport as a Schr\"{o}dinger Bridge on the hypersphere and establish the log-density-ratio limit of the resulting steering field; (ii) we introduce Conditional Optimal Bridge for Riemannian Activation Steering (\Cobras), combining transport-based sample weighting, alignment-based strength control, and support gating, and develop a simplified external gate applicable to existing steering methods; and (iii) we demonstrate improvements over prior baselines in nearly all evaluated model--task settings spanning helpfulness, truthfulness, and detoxification, while largely preserving OOD capabilities.
